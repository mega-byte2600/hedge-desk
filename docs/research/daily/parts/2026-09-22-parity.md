## Box-spread parity literature & practitioner evidence

- **Hemler & Miller (1997), "Box Spread Arbitrage Profits Following the 1987 Market Crash" (SSRN)** — classic: box-spread parity on SPX; pre-crash violations rare and unprofitable with execution delay; post-crash violations frequent and profitable even with 5-min delay → measured market-efficiency *decrease*. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=8271 — **fact** (peer-reviewed, JFQA 1997)
- **SET50 index options box-spread test (ResearchGate; Lertburapa 2015 / Jongadsayakul 2016)** — after transaction costs, box-spread violations <1%, none persisting to the next trading day; put-call-futures parity violations 4–7% raw. Benchmark for what a "clean" box-parity screen should find in an efficient market. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/309671497_A_Box_Spread_Test_of_the_SET50_Index_Options_Market_Efficiency_Evidence_from_the_Thailand_Futures_Exchange — **fact** (peer-reviewed claims; verify via the paper itself before citing precise numbers)
- **OCC, "Box Spreads" educational paper (2025)** — OCC's own current paper: boxes require European-style options to preserve the defined outcome; implied rates track T-bill rates; OCC guarantees the trade. Freshest official-word document on the instrument. https://www.optionseducation.org/getmedia/1dcd759e-d52b-4323-8095-c3a343cab824/Box-Spreads-Paper_2025.pdf — **fact**
- **Wesley Gray (Alpha Architect), "Box Spreads: An Alternative to Treasury Bills?"** — practitioner explainer with worked SPX box example; frames box-implied rate vs. 1-yr T-bill. https://medium.com/@alphaarchitect/box-spreads-an-alternative-to-treasury-bills-b86dc896ca71 — **fact** (industry blog, illustrative not empirical)
- **Bogleheads "Box Spreads as Loans" mega-thread (2021–ongoing)** — years of practitioner-reported realized box-implied rates; consensus observation is a persistent ~20–40 bp premium over comparable T-bills, attributed to illiquidity/convenience of Treasuries rather than exploitable arbitrage; also documents realized trading costs (~0.1–0.15% p.a. all-in on short-dated boxes). https://www.bogleheads.org/forum/viewtopic.php?t=344667&sid=0a7408665bb0aa05b0ccfed64b685399&start=550 — **fact** (practitioner data, not academic)

## Notable findings for the desk

1. **OESX is structurally box-friendly**: European-style exercise + cash settlement removes the two main American-option box hazards (early exercise, assignment). The parity relation a box test needs is exactly what OESX provides. **fact from specs**
2. **The 11:50–12:00 CET settlement averaging window matters**: OESX final settlement is a 10-minute average, not a closing print. Any box-parity observation near expiry must compare option quotes against the *average-based* settlement value, or the screen will manufacture phantom "violations." **inference from spec facts**
3. **Realistic expectation for the screen**: the empirical literature (SET50; post-1987 SPX) says genuine box violations in a liquid European index options market are rare (<1%) and non-persistent once frictions are applied. If the desk's scanner flags frequent "dislocations," the first suspect should be stale quotes, tick-size effects, or the settlement-window mismatch above — not arbitrage. **inference from literature**
4. **Box-implied financing rates are a usable desk output on their own**: practitioner data shows box-implied rates tracking T-bills within tens of bps. Publishing a daily box-implied-rate series for OESX (vs. €STR) would be a legitimate paper-only research product for this desk even when no violations exist. **inference / suggestion**
5. **Gap**: no recent (post-2020) peer-reviewed box-parity study on *European* index options specifically turned up in this light sweep — most empirical work is SPX or Asian markets. Worth flagging as a known hole if the desk wants an academic anchor for OESX. **fact about the search**
